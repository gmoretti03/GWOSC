\paragraph{What the comparison supports.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The frequentist and Bayesian statistics are two views of the same matched-filter cube and detect the same bursts; the Bayesian analysis adds calibrated posteriors and the prior dependence of the evidence, the frequentist analysis adds optimality statements (Neyman--Pearson oracle gap) and guaranteed coverage.
\item A network with no template knowledge reaches matched-filter performance on the family the bank was designed for, and exceeds it on the shapes it has seen; trained with fresh injections at every epoch a single CNN does better than a five-member ensemble trained on a fixed set. Its advantage is bounded by its training set: on a morphology it has not seen it falls below the bank, so it is not a replacement for a morphology-independent statistic.
\item \textbf{Which morphology?} Model comparison with the exact likelihood of each family (Bayes factors) is the best classifier from $\snr\approx6$, the CNN is tied with it at high SNR and $0.04$--$0.05$ behind at $\snr=5$--6, and no method can say that a signal belongs to none of the families.
\item \textbf{Sequence structure does not help here.} A patch Transformer and a GRU detect worse than the CNN with every lever tried (data, learning rate, patch size, longer training). The patch embedding, not the data or the optimisation, is the limitation: a convolutional stem recovers most of the gap, but the CNN stays ahead on one-second windows with a localised burst.
\item None of the statistics escapes the real noise: the glitch floor dominates the price of a low false-alarm probability, and the calibration of a threshold is a property of the epoch in which it was measured.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What it does not support.} These are efficiencies for single-detector injections into one-second windows at a given \emph{optimal SNR}; they are not astrophysical sensitivities, not rates, and say nothing about coincidence between detectors, where most of the real-world rejection of glitches comes from.
