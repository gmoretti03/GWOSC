\subsection{Why does the CNN beat the bank?}\label{sec:ablation}
Notebook~15 tests three explanations on the three phenomenological morphologies (thresholds from the validation background, mean $\snr_{50}$ over sine-Gaussian, pulse and chirp, bootstrap error $\approx0.1$ at FAP $10^{-2}$, $\approx0.35$ at $10^{-3}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}).

\begin{figure}[h]\centering\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{fig15_ablation.pdf}\caption{Mean $\snr_{50}$ of the ablation cases of Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.}\label{fig:ablation}\end{figure}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{A richer bank does not close the gap} ($5.54$ against $5.58$): the advantage is not template coverage.
\item \emph{In Gaussian noise the network is not better than the bank}: $5.63$ against $5.38$ at $10^{-2}$ (it is better on pulses, worse on sine-Gaussians), where it was never trained. The CNN advantage of $\approx1.1$ ($10^{-2}$) and $\approx2.2$ ($10^{-3}$) seen in real noise is therefore largely an advantage on \emph{real} noise, in which the network was trained: the real noise costs the bank $0.2$ at $10^{-2}$ and $1.7$ at $10^{-3}$ and costs the network nothing. Part of it is a domain effect (the network was not retrained in Gaussian noise), so this is evidence that the advantage lives in the tail of real noise, not a proof of its mechanism.
\item \emph{A crude glitch veto recovers part of the gap at $10^{-3}$} ($7.71\to6.87$ on the morphologies that still reach $50\,\%$) and nothing at $10^{-2}$; signal-consistency vetoes ($\chi^2$) are the natural next step.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Generalisation to O2 and O3}\label{sec:o2o3}
Notebook~36 applies the unchanged pipeline (own-chunk median-Welch PSD, one-second windows, band $30$--$500\,$Hz) to nine new H1 chunks, three each in O2, O3a and O3b ($\ge2000\,$s from catalogued events). The in-band amplitude spectral density is $0.91$ (O2), $0.65$ (O3a) and $0.61$ (O3b) times that of O1, but the data are much less Gaussian in O3: the excess kurtosis of the whitened samples is $10^{5}$--$10^{6}$ and the largest bank statistics reach $7\cdot10^{5}$ (a single window of $\rho\approx800$), orders of magnitude above O1.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{The O1 thresholds transfer remarkably well at FAP $10^{-2}$}: the observed FAP is $0.9$--$1.6\,\%$ for the bank and $1.4$--$2.1\,\%$ for the CNN, a factor $<2$ off the nominal value, and the $\snr_{50}$ with the O1 threshold is the same as in O1 ($5.55$ for the bank; $3.7$--$3.9$ for the five-network CNN ensemble, against $3.79$ in O1, Section~\ref{sec:seeds}): the whitening with the chunk's own PSD absorbs the change of sensitivity.
\item \textbf{At FAP $10^{-3}$ the tails decide}: with its own threshold the bank in O3 needs a network SNR above $16$ for $50\,\%$ efficiency, because three O3 chunks contain windows with a statistic of $10^{4}$--$10^{6}$ that push the $99.9\,\%$ quantile above any realistic signal; the CNN is far less affected ($5.4$--$6.2$). In O3 the data-quality flags of Section~\ref{sec:dq} (not applied here) or time slides are indispensable for a template statistic.
\item The CNN trained on O1 only keeps its ranking with the bank in O2 and O3 without retraining ($3.7$--$4.5$ against $5.5$--$5.9$ at $10^{-2}$): its advantage does not depend on the epoch of the training noise.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the comparison supports.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The frequentist and Bayesian statistics are two views of the same matched-filter cube and detect the same bursts; the Bayesian analysis adds calibrated posteriors and the prior dependence of the evidence, the frequentist analysis adds optimality statements (Neyman--Pearson oracle gap) and guaranteed coverage.
\item A network with no template knowledge reaches matched-filter performance on the family the bank was designed for, and exceeds it on the shapes it has seen; trained with fresh injections at every epoch a single CNN does better than a five-member ensemble trained on a fixed set. Its advantage is bounded by its training set: on a morphology it has not seen it falls below the bank, so it is not a replacement for a morphology-independent statistic.
\item \textbf{Realistic signals.} On binary-black-hole signals the detectors designed on phenomenological bursts do not transfer for free (the sine-Gaussian bank loses $2.0$ in $\snr_{50}$ with respect to an IMR bank, excess power $2.9$); the networks trained on the phenomenological morphologies generalise better than the banks of the wrong family, and a network trained with IMR signals beats the IMR bank on waveforms never seen in training, at no cost on the other morphologies.
\item \textbf{Two detectors.} Coincidence with time slides moves the false-alarm floor from $10^{-3}$ (one detector, set by the glitch tail) to $10^{-6}$ per window at a cost of a few SNR units; the CNN and the matched filter of the right family then both need a network SNR of $\approx11$. The combination statistic must be robust to a glitch in one detector.
\item \textbf{Which morphology?} Model comparison with the exact likelihood of each family (Bayes factors) is the best classifier among the networks trained on the static set; a CNN trained with fresh injections ties with it at low SNR and is slightly ahead at high SNR ($0.977$ against $0.969$ at $\snr=8$). A signal that belongs to none of the families is not flagged by the family decision of any method, but it is by an explicit goodness-of-fit of the matched filter (stochastic bursts) or by the energy score of the network (chirps of the wrong family).
\item \textbf{Sequence structure does not help here.} A patch Transformer and a GRU detect worse than the CNN with every lever tried (data, learning rate, patch size, longer training). The patch embedding, not the data or the optimisation, is the limitation: a convolutional stem recovers most of the gap, but the CNN stays ahead on one-second windows with a localised burst.
\item None of the statistics escapes the real noise: the glitch floor dominates the price of a low false-alarm probability, and the calibration of a threshold is a property of the epoch in which it was measured.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What it does not support.} These are efficiencies for injections into one-second windows at a given \emph{optimal SNR}; they are not astrophysical sensitivities and not rates. Even the two-detector analysis lacks a coherent network statistic, sky localisation, signal-consistency vetoes and the very low false-alarm rates of real searches.
