\section{Conclusions and limits}\label{sec:conclusions}

\paragraph{Conclusions.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{The real noise is the main result.} Whitened O1 H1 data are Gaussian in the bulk, but a glitch floor of a few $10^{-3}$ per second and strong epoch-to-epoch variations decide the price of a false-alarm probability: lowering it from $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-3}$ moves the 50\,\% point of the bank from $\snr\approx5.5$ to $\approx11$ with the training-background threshold and from $4.9$ to $6.8$ with the validation one (in Gaussian noise the threshold would move only from $\approx27$ to $\approx31$, i.e.\ by $\approx0.4$ in SNR), and a threshold calibrated on one set of epochs is off by a factor $\approx4.5$ on others.
\item \textbf{Frequentist.} The profile-likelihood bank reaches 50\,\% efficiency at $\snr\approx5$ (oracle with known parameters: $3.0$); the gap is the look-elsewhere penalty, quantified by $N_{\rm eff}\approx6800$ independent trials. The MLE saturates the Fisher bound for $\snr\gtrsim10$; the amplitude estimate is biased and the profile interval under-covers near the boundary, where Neyman/Feldman--Cousins intervals are exact. The dominant analysis systematic is the PSD estimator (mean against median Welch: $+0.025$ on $\varepsilon(\snr=8)$).
\item \textbf{Bayesian.} The exact grid posterior is validated against a closed form, MCMC and simulation-based calibration, in Gaussian and in real noise; the Bayes factor ranks windows like $\lmax$ but is prior-dependent (Occam factor $-2\ln\sigma_\rho$) and exceeds 100 on $0.2$--$0.9\,\%$ of pure-noise windows because of glitches. Credible intervals match confidence intervals for $\snr\gtrsim3$ and differ, depending on the prior, below.
\item \textbf{Machine learning.} A small CNN ensemble matches the bank on sine-Gaussians and beats it on the pulses and chirps it was trained on by about one SNR unit, but a network trained on one morphology fails on others; an autoencoder trained on noise adds nothing to an energy statistic. Calibration is good ($\mathrm{ECE}\lesssim3\,\%$), mostly limited by the epoch shift, and ensemble uncertainty identifies the windows where the model should not be trusted.
\item \textbf{Sequence models and tuning.} A Transformer and a GRU detect worse than the CNN, with the same data and with every lever tried; the training loss of the Transformer stays high ($\approx0.3$ against $0.12$ for the CNN): the patch embedding cannot build the frequency-selective filters a burst needs. A convolutional stem in front of the Transformer recovers most of the gap but does not overtake the CNN. The largest effect of the whole study is on the CNN itself, which was data-limited: fresh injections at every epoch make a single network better than the five-member ensemble ($\overline{\snr_{50}}=4.6$ against $4.85$ at FAP $10^{-2}$).
\item \textbf{Which morphology.} Bayesian model comparison is the best classifier among the networks trained on the static set ($0.97$ three-way accuracy at $\snr=8$); a CNN trained with fresh injections ties with it at low SNR and is slightly ahead at high SNR ($0.977$), so the ranking of Section~\ref{sec:seq} was a statement about a data-limited network.
\item \textbf{Realistic signals.} The detectors designed on phenomenological bursts do not transfer for free to binary-black-hole (IMRPhenomD) signals: the sine-Gaussian bank loses $2.0$ in $\snr_{50}$ with respect to an IMR bank ($7.4$ against $5.4$ at FAP $10^{-2}$; oracle $3.0$); a CNN trained with IMR signals reaches $4.45$ on waveforms never seen in training, at no cost on the other morphologies.
\item \textbf{Two detectors.} Coincidence with time slides moves the false-alarm floor from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-6}$ per window at a cost of $3.7$--$6.9$ in network SNR (CNN and IMR bank: $\snr_{50}\approx11$ at $10^{-6}$); the noises of H1 and L1 are independent (zero-lag against slides of the same chunks: ratio $0.6$--$1.1$), but a combination statistic must be robust to a glitch in one detector.
\item \textbf{Unknown morphology.} The goodness-of-fit of the matched filter (residual after subtracting the best fit) flags stochastic bursts (AUROC $0.94$ at $\snr=8$, $\ge0.998$ at $\snr\ge12$) and the energy score of the network flags chirps of the wrong family ($0.93$ at $\snr=8$); the Mahalanobis distance of the features fails.
\item \textbf{What the second round of extensions added.} (i) Errors: the bootstrap error of $\snr_{50}$ is $0.05$--$0.1$ at FAP $10^{-2}$, but the seed scatter of the CNN is $0.4$ (five seeds), so the ranking CNN $<$ hybrid $<$ Transformer ($1$--$2$ units) is robust and differences of tenths between single-seed networks are not. (ii) The advantage of the CNN over the bank lives in the tails of the real noise and in the injection prior: in Gaussian noise the bank is as good, a richer bank does not help, a $\chi^2$ test and the GWOSC CAT2 flags recover most of the gap at FAP $10^{-3}$ for the bank, and the CNN loses its advantage outside its training ranges ($\snr_{50}$ $8.6$ against $5.1$ at $400$--$500\,$Hz, $0.02$ efficiency on loud short pulses) and under a $60\,$Hz line (FAP $0.4\to17\,\%$). (iii) Physical units: $h_{\rm rss,50}\approx2.5\cdot10^{-23}\,\mathrm{Hz}^{-1/2}$ for single H1, a Euclidean range of $1.3$--$1.6\,$Gpc for $30$--$100\,M_\odot$, and with real antenna patterns the network $\snr_{50}$ at FAP $10^{-6}$ is $\approx15$, not $11$; the coherent statistic tried here brings no gain. (iv) Parameters: $\approx7\,\%$ in $M_c$ and $M$ for the bank, the grid posterior and the exact-model MCMC alike, with calibrated intervals only for the MCMC and the CNN; hierarchical inference with selection effects recovers a mass distribution that unfolding and naive fits do not. (v) Generalisation: the O1 calibration transfers to O2 and O3 to a factor $<2$, the CNN trained on O1 keeps its ranking, and the glitches of O3 make the bank unusable at $10^{-3}$ without data-quality vetoes; a normalising flow or a VAE trained on noise only costs $\approx2$ in $\snr_{50}$ with no prior on the morphology.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Limits.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Detectors.} Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:imr} use H1 alone, so their false-alarm numbers are per one-second window of a single detector, not rates; Section~\ref{sec:coinc} adds L1 with an incoherent combination (no coherent statistic, no antenna-pattern consistency, no $\chi^2$ vetoes) and six chunks, down to $10^{-6}$ per window.
\item \textbf{Injections at a given SNR.} Not converted into distances or energies. The three morphologies are phenomenological; the IMRPhenomD library covers total masses $30$--$100\,M_\odot$, aligned spins only (no precession, higher modes or eccentricity), and neutron-star binaries are out of reach of one-second windows and a 500\,Hz band (Section~\ref{sec:limits}: $9\,\%$ of the SNR of a $1.4+1.4\,M_\odot$ system is retained); precessing, higher-mode and eccentric signals cost at most $0.3$--$0.9$ in $\snr_{50}$ and NSBH signals $5.5$ for the IMR bank.
\item \textbf{Background statistics.} $1.6\times10^4$ training windows limit the lowest usable FAP to $\sim10^{-3}$; the quoted $10^{-3}$ efficiencies carry a large uncertainty from the tail of the background.
\item \textbf{Eight chunks.} The epoch dependence is measured with two test chunks; a systematic study of its size would need many more.
\item \textbf{Grid resolution.} The Bayesian grid under-resolves $f_0$ and $t_0$ for $\snr\gtrsim15$ (the SBC coverage of those two parameters, $0.50$--$0.60$, reflects this, not the model).
\item \textbf{ML numbers} are efficiencies \emph{for the training injection prior}; the supervised detectors are only as good as the injections they have seen, and parameter regression with the CNN (Section~\ref{sec:pe}) is $10$--$17\,\%$ in $M_c$, worse than the bank.
\item \textbf{Tuning study.} Single seed for most variants (notebooks 26, 34, 39: the seed scatter of the CNN is $0.4$ at FAP $10^{-2}$), a budget of 12--30 epochs; contrastive pre-training and overlapping windows for the Transformer not run.
\item \textbf{Comparison thresholds.} The bank is compared with the ML detectors on the validation background; the choice of background changes the numbers by up to $0.6$ in $\snr_{50}$ (Section~\ref{sec:ml}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Natural extensions.} A full coherent network analysis (sky grid, both polarisations, null stream, $\chi^2$ vetoes) and three-detector time slides; windows of $4$--$8\,$s or $16\,$kHz data for low masses and neutron stars; nested sampling with spins, sky position and several detectors; training the networks with a glitch class, an explicit unknown class and a wider injection prior with no input clipping; more seeds for every variant; contrastive pre-training of the Transformer; the LVK data-quality flags and auxiliary-channel vetoes in the template statistic.
