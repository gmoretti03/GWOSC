\section{Reproducibility and origin of the numbers}\label{app:code}

The project is a pipeline of notebooks in \texttt{Code\_General/}, run in order with the Jupyter kernel \texttt{cern} (environment in \texttt{environment.yml}); every random number is generated with a fixed seed and long steps cache their output.
\path{download_data.py} downloads the eight raw chunks (about 0.5\,GB in float32; not tracked); \path{gwosc_tools.py} holds the shared functions (whitening, analytic waveforms, injection, template bank, statistics), \path{seq_models.py} the models, the dataset and the resumable training loop of the tuning study (it trains the variants in parallel processes), \path{imr_tools.py} the IMRPhenomD library and the IMR template bank (it needs \texttt{lalsuite} only to build the libraries, \texttt{python imr\_tools.py}), \path{open_set.py} the family fits and goodness-of-fit scores, and \path{download_coinc_data.py} the six H1+L1 chunks of Section~\ref{sec:coinc}.
Run times on a 10-core laptop (on AC power): notebook 01 $\approx$1\,min, 02 $\approx$1\,min, 03 $\approx$10--15\,min (most of it the systematic variants), 04 $\approx$20\,min, 05 $\approx$30--40\,min with training from scratch (a few minutes with the saved models), 06 $\approx$1--2\,h, 07 $\approx$3\,h of training on four processes in total, 08 $\approx$10\,min plus 20\,min for the two networks trained with IMR signals, 09 $\approx$10\,min plus the download of the L1 data, 10 $\approx$10\,min (a laptop on battery power runs about three times slower).
