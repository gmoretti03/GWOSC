\section{Machine-learning detectors}\label{sec:ml}

\subsection{Data set and models}
The labelled set is built from the same chunks and the same split: for each noise window of a split we also build a signal window, the \emph{same} window with a burst whose morphology is drawn uniformly among the three and whose optimal SNR is uniform in $[3,15]$. Classes are balanced and the label-0 windows contain all the real glitches and non-stationarity
($16\,224$ training and $8112$ validation windows per class, $8112$ test windows). A second training set contains only sine-Gaussians, to test generalisation to morphologies not seen in training. The networks see the whitened series clipped to $\pm12$ (glitches reach hundreds of $\sigma$); the feature pipeline uses the unclipped series.

\paragraph{Feature-based models.} Twenty-two morphology-agnostic features: the maximum over time of the z-scored sliding energy at five durations (the excess-power statistic at each scale), the maximum and the standard deviation over time of the energy in seven frequency sub-bands (30--500\,Hz, from a short-time spectrogram), and max\,$|v|$, kurtosis and std/MAD of the window. No matched-filter output is used.
\paragraph{CNN.} A one-dimensional convolutional network of $17\,761$ parameters: three Conv--BatchNorm--ReLU blocks (kernels 15, 9, 5; 16, 32, 64 channels; max-pooling by 4), global max-pooling (translation invariance: the burst can be anywhere), a dense layer with dropout $0.2$ and one logit; binary cross-entropy, Adam~\cite{Adam}, batch 128, early stopping on the validation loss (14 epochs). The \emph{ensemble} is five independently initialised copies (the logits are averaged); a second network is trained on sine-Gaussians only.
\paragraph{Autoencoder.} A convolutional autoencoder ($3.6\times10^5$ parameters, 32 latent values for 1024 samples) trained on the real noise windows \emph{only}; the reconstruction error is a morphology-independent anomaly score.

\subsection{Regularisation, bias--variance and what the features say}
Logistic regression with $L_1$ and $L_2$ penalties (Fig.~\ref{fig:reg}) reaches a validation AUC of $0.927$ with all features; the 5-fold cross-validated $L_1$ choice $C=0.019$ keeps 18 of the 22 features and costs nothing in AUC, while for $C\lesssim10^{-3}$ the $L_1$ model collapses much faster than $L_2$ (it sets coefficients to zero one by one).
Trees (Fig.~\ref{fig:bv}): the validation AUC of gradient boosting saturates at $0.938$ for depth 4--6 while the training AUC keeps rising ($0.936\to0.956$ from depth 1 to 12): beyond depth $\sim4$ there is only variance. The learning curve (depth 4) starts at a training AUC of $1.0$ against $0.925$ on validation with 500 examples, and the two approach each other ($0.952$ / $0.938$) at $3.2\times10^4$ examples without having converged: the feature models are data-limited, which is the practical argument for giving the network the raw series.
The most important features by permutation importance are the 10\,ms excess-power score and the energy in the 50--180\,Hz bands; the first principal component of the features carries 46\,\% of the variance. Validation AUCs: ridge $0.927$, random forest $0.938$, gradient boosting $0.938$.

\subsection{Comparison with the frequentist statistics on the same injections}
All statistics are evaluated on exactly the same test windows and injections, with the threshold of each set to the 99\,\% quantile of its own distribution on the \emph{same} background, the validation noise (chunks 1 and 5). A common background is what makes the comparison fair: Section~\ref{sec:freq} used the training chunks, whose glitchy chunk~2 gives a more conservative bank threshold
(there $\snr_{50}=5.5$ for sine-Gaussians; here $4.9$). Table~\ref{tab:mlcomp} and Fig.~\ref{fig:mleff} summarise the result; if the threshold is instead set on the test noise itself (equal observed FAP) the networks gain $0.4$--$0.6$ in $\snr_{50}$ (the test epochs are cleaner than the validation ones) and the bank only $0.04$, so that the ensemble then also beats the bank in family ($4.6$ against $4.8$); the conclusions for pulse and chirp and for the unseen morphology are unchanged.

Gradient boosting on 22 features is already at the bank level.
\item \textbf{A morphology not seen in training.} The CNN trained on sine-Gaussians only loses efficiency exactly where its training stops: $\snr_{50}=6.5$ (pulse) and $6.8$ (chirp) against $5.3$ and $6.3$ for the bank, and the efficiency at $\snr=8$ falls from $0.98$ to $0.71$ and $0.70$ (bank: $0.99$, $0.88$). The advantage of a supervised detector is bought with training data; outside the training distribution the bank, which has no data to fail on, is the more robust.
\item \textbf{Unsupervised.} The reconstruction error of the whole window is the total energy of a second of almost white noise --- there is nothing to learn --- and is inefficient ($\snr_{50}\approx12$). The \emph{local} maximum of the error recovers the performance of excess power ($6.9$, $6.8$, $8.3$) but not more: an autoencoder can beat an energy statistic only if the noise has structure that it can learn, which after whitening it hardly has.
\item \textbf{The CNN of this section is data-limited.} Section~\ref{sec:tuning} shows that training it with fresh injections at every epoch improves it by $\approx0.5$--$0.7$ in $\snr_{50}$ (a single network then beats the five-member ensemble); the numbers of this section are those of the static-data network, kept as the reference of the comparison.
\item \textbf{False alarms.} On the test chunks the observed FAP at a nominal $1\,\%$ is $0.85\,\%$ (bank), $0.92\,\%$ (excess power), $0.75\,\%$ (gradient boosting), $0.53\,\%$ (ensemble) and $0.35\,\%$ (single CNN): no statistic is immune to the epoch dependence of the background.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Calibration and uncertainty}
A score is useful to a physicist if it is a probability. On balanced validation and test sets (noise plus mixed injections with SNR 3--15) all models are close to calibrated (Fig.~\ref{fig:cal}): the expected calibration error is $0.7\,\%$ for gradient boosting, $2.2$--$2.4\,\%$ for the CNNs and $2.7\,\%$ for the ridge model on the test set. The CNNs are slightly \emph{under}-confident at intermediate scores (observed signal fraction up to $\approx0.15$ above the prediction between $0.1$ and $0.7$).
Temperature scaling~\cite{Calibration} fitted on validation does not help ($T=0.94$--$1.02$; ECE changes by at most $0.002$): the miscalibration is not of the temperature type. For the CNNs the ECE is $0.8$--$1.2\,\%$ on the validation epochs they were tuned on and $2.2$--$2.4\,\%$ on the test epochs, so most of it is an \emph{epoch shift}. The ensemble is better than a single network in every proper score
(Brier $0.0350$ against $0.0413$, log-loss $0.110$ against $0.128$, AUC $0.9938$ against $0.9909$); the AUC on the test epochs is higher than on the validation ones for every model (ensemble $0.988\to0.994$), because the test chunks are cleaner: no sign of over-fitting to the training epochs.

The epistemic uncertainty is estimated with the ensemble~\cite{DeepEnsembles} and with MC dropout~\cite{MCDropout} ($30$ stochastic passes). The ensemble spread falls with SNR (mean std $0.16$ at $\snr\approx4$, $\approx0.001$ for $\snr\ge12$) and peaks at $p\approx0.5$; MC dropout shows the same trend but is only moderately correlated with it ($0.35$): they measure different things
(spread over dropout masks of one network against disagreement of independently trained networks). Rejecting the $20\,\%$ most uncertain windows raises the AUC from $0.9924$ to $0.9997$.

\subsection{Hard-negative mining and families of real glitches}\label{sec:glitchfam}
\paragraph{Mining.} Notebook~17 fine-tunes the fresh-injection CNN for $8$ epochs either as is (control) or repeating $5$ times, at every epoch, the $10\,\%$ of training noise windows with the highest network score. Mean $\snr_{50}$ over the three morphologies (validation thresholds): baseline $4.57/5.66$, control $4.37/5.80$, mining $4.46/5.84$ at FAP $10^{-2}/10^{-3}$. The paired difference mining\,$-$\,control is $+0.10$ ($95\,\%$ interval $-0.15$ to $+0.33$) and $+0.04$ ($-0.21$ to $+0.33$): no measurable effect. With a binary cross-entropy that already sees $16\,000$ noise windows per epoch, the hard windows are not rare enough for re-weighting to matter at FAP $\ge10^{-3}$; the effect might appear at lower FAP, which the available background cannot test.
\paragraph{Families.} Notebook~18 takes the $4\,\%$ of validation and test windows with the largest bank statistic ($649$), describes them by their log-spectrogram and clusters them (k-means, $3$ families plus an ``extreme'' family of the $3\,\%$ most atypical windows). The silhouette of the typical windows is only $0.08$: they are noise-tail fluctuations that differ essentially by the band in which the bank fired (peak frequency $113$, $218$ and $280\,$Hz), not distinct glitch classes. The extreme family ($20$ windows, median $\max|v|=4.7$) is the real-glitch population: the bank alarms in $95\,\%$ of them at FAP $10^{-2}$, the $\chi^2<3$ condition removes $\approx95\,\%$ of those alarms and the CNN alarms in $5\,\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:glitchfam}). Without human labels the unsupervised analysis therefore supports one conclusion: what separates the detectors is a small tail of loud glitches that the template statistic cannot reject by amplitude and that the network and the $\chi^2$ test do.

\subsection{Prior mismatch and the base rate}\label{sec:priorshift}
\paragraph{Prior mismatch.} Notebook~20 tests the fresh-injection CNN and the $160$-template bank on injections outside the training ranges (FAP $10^{-2}$, validation thresholds; Fig.~\ref{fig:priorbase}). The bank keeps its performance ($\snr_{50}\le7.0$ in every case, and efficiency $1.0$ at $\snr=40$ everywhere), the CNN does not:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item sine-Gaussians at $400$--$500\,$Hz: $\snr_{50}=8.6$ for the CNN against $5.1$ for the bank (efficiency at $\snr=8$: $0.42$ against $0.99$); at $30$--$50\,$Hz $6.7$ against $5.7$;
\item pulses of $5$--$15\,$ms: $9.3$ against $6.7$; chirps at $150$--$250\,$Hz: $7.4$ against $7.0$;
\item loud signals: the efficiency at $\snr=40$ of the CNN falls to $0.72$ for pulses in the training range, $0.02$ for pulses of $0.3$--$0.8\,$ms and $0.65$ for chirps at $150$--$250\,$Hz, because the network input is clipped at $\pm12$ and the training SNR stops at $15$: the loudest events are the ones it misses;
\item mild extrapolations (broader or narrower $Q$, steeper chirps) are harmless: $4.6$--$5.3$ for the CNN.
\end{itemize}
The CNN advantage of this section is therefore an advantage \emph{inside the injection prior}; outside it, the template statistic degrades gracefully and the network does not. The remedy (a wider prior and no clipping, or a bank veto for loud events) is not tested here.
\paragraph{Base rate.} The efficiencies of this report describe a balanced problem. If a one-second window contains a signal with prior probability $\pi$, an alarm is real with probability $\mathrm{PPV}=\varepsilon\pi/(\varepsilon\pi+\mathrm{FAP}(1-\pi))$. With $\varepsilon\approx0.99$ at $\snr=8$ and for an \emph{illustrative} rate of one detectable burst per day ($\pi\approx1.2\cdot10^{-5}$) the PPV is $0.001$ at a single-detector FAP of $10^{-2}$, $0.01$ at $10^{-3}$ and $0.90$ at the two-detector $10^{-6}$ of Section~\ref{sec:coinc}; at one per month it is $4\cdot10^{-5}$, $3\cdot10^{-4}$ and $0.24$. A PPV of $0.9$ needs a FAP of $10^{-6}$ (one per day) or $4\cdot10^{-8}$ (one per month): the single-detector FAPs quoted in this report are far from sufficient for a real search, which is why the two-detector time slides are essential. The rates are assumptions, not measured burst rates.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item A $\pm10\,\%$ error on the calibration of the signal amplitude moves the efficiency at $\snr=6$ by about $\pm0.1$--$0.15$ for both detectors, as any $\snr$ rescaling does; the alternative PSD estimates change the FAP by a factor $<1.5$ and the efficiencies by $<0.02$ for both.
\item \textbf{A spectral line left in the data breaks the CNN, not the bank}: with a $60\,$Hz line of norm $3$ the CNN FAP rises from $0.4\,\%$ to $17\,\%$ ($29\,\%$ at norm $6$), the bank is unchanged. The network has never seen a persistent line and responds to a feature that the template statistic, with its narrow-band but short-lived templates, ignores. Retraining with augmentation and the robust tests of Section~\ref{sec:priorshift} are the remedies; the line itself is removed in practice by the PSD whitening.
\item The bank cannot see signals whose lag lies outside the range it scans ($0.15$--$0.85\,$s), an explicit design choice, while the CNN keeps $0.74$ out of its training prior of the lag.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Conformal prediction.} A split-conformal threshold (the $\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th calibration score) is guaranteed to give FAP $\le\alpha$ for exchangeable data; across epochs it is not: calibrating on one validation chunk and testing on the other gives observed/nominal ratios of $0.5$--$1.9$ for the CNN and $0.5$--$3.5$ for the bank at $\alpha=10^{-2},10^{-3}$, i.e.\ the guarantee holds only within an epoch (this is the epoch dependence of Section~\ref{sec:bkgrigour} seen from another side). Conformal sets of the 4-class morphology network at $90\,\%$ nominal coverage have an average size $1.00$--$1.04$ and marginal coverage $0.98$ on noise, but the \emph{conditional} coverage depends on the SNR (sine-Gaussian: $0.07$ at $\snr=3$, $0.69$ at $6$, $0.95$ at $8$): a faint signal receives a confident wrong label rather than a larger set, and the $90\,\%$ holds only on average over the training SNR prior.
\paragraph{Interpretability.} The input gradient times the input of the detection logit puts $30\,\%$ of its mass within $\pm4\sigma_t$ of an injected sine-Gaussian, against $10\,\%$ for a uniform saliency; the $16$ first-layer filters peak between $\approx40$ and $500\,$Hz (Fig.~\ref{fig:interp}), covering the injection prior as a filter bank would.
\begin{figure}[h]\centering\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{fig25_interpretability.pdf}\caption{Left: spectra of the $16$ first-layer kernels. Centre: their peak frequencies against the sine-Gaussian prior. Right: saliency of twenty injected signals.}\label{fig:interp}\end{figure}
\paragraph{Latency.} Per one-second window, the CNN takes $0.17\,$ms in batches of $1024$ (and $88\,$ms for a single call, Keras overhead), the hybrid $1.9\,$ms, the Transformer $1.0\,$ms and the $160$-template bank $3.5\,$ms (numpy FFT): the network is $20$ times faster than the bank on a batch.
\paragraph{Adversarial perturbations.} A one-step FGSM attack with $\epsilon=0.1\sigma$ per sample (perturbation norm $3.2$, a third of the signal norm $10$) lowers the CNN efficiency at $\snr=10$ from $1.0$ to $0.33$ ($0.02$ at $0.2\sigma$), while the bank, given the same perturbation, stays at $0.998$; in the opposite direction $\epsilon=0.05\sigma$ takes the CNN FAP from $0.5\,\%$ to $7\,\%$. The CNN is not adversarially robust, which matters only if the noise could be chosen by an adversary; it matters in practice as a statement about sensitivity to small coherent structure.

\subsection{Unsupervised noise models: a normalising flow and a variational autoencoder}\label{sec:flowvae}
Notebook~33 uses models of the noise only (no signal is ever seen) as model-independent detectors. A RealNVP flow ($6$ affine coupling layers) learns the density of a $16$-dimensional description of each window ($8$ log band energies, the maxima of the sliding energy at $5$ durations, $\max|v|$, skewness, kurtosis); its score is $-\ln p$. A $1$-D convolutional VAE (latent dimension $16$) reconstructs the whitened samples; scores are the largest local ($50\,$ms) reconstruction error and the window ELBO. Thresholds are those of the validation noise.

The alarms of the unsupervised models are not simply the glitches that fool the bank: only $20$--$26\,\%$ of the bank alarms are also flagged (Spearman correlation of the scores with the bank statistic $0.17$--$0.33$), so they are complementary background rejectors rather than copies of the matched filter.
